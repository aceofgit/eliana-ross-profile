Short Bio

Eliana Ross is a Sydney-based somatic wellbeing facilitator, breathwork practitioner and mindfulness teacher who creates thoughtful experiences designed to help people slow down, reconnect with themselves and build more sustainable ways of living and working.

Her work brings together evidence-informed nervous-system education, guided breathwork, mindfulness, gentle movement, reflective practices and intentional conversation. Eliana works with both individuals and organisations, facilitating private sessions, workplace wellbeing programs, team workshops, retreats and immersive group experiences.

Her approach is warm, grounded and practical, making wellbeing accessible without stripping it of depth.

Full Bio

For more than a decade, Eliana Ross has explored the relationship between wellbeing, creativity, human connection and the environments in which we live and work.

Originally working in the worlds of hospitality and creative production, Eliana became increasingly interested in what enables people to feel genuinely present, energised and connected rather than simply productive. That curiosity eventually led her into formal study and training across mindfulness, breathwork, somatic practices, restorative movement and group facilitation.

Today, she creates experiences for individuals, teams and communities that invite people to step out of habitual patterns and return to a greater sense of awareness.

Eliana's sessions are intentionally experiential. Rather than asking participants to absorb large amounts of theory, she combines simple explanations with practices that can be felt in the body and integrated into everyday life.

A typical experience might move between guided breathing, gentle mobility, sensory awareness, reflective writing and small-group conversation. Depending on the setting, sessions can be quiet and restorative or energetic, social and highly participatory.

Her corporate work focuses particularly on sustainable performance, nervous-system regulation, communication, attention and recovery. She has facilitated sessions for leadership teams, creative agencies, hospitality groups, professional-services firms, founders and organisations navigating periods of rapid growth or change.

Alongside organisational work, Eliana supports individuals through one-to-one sessions, small-group programs, retreats and seasonal workshops.

She believes wellbeing does not need to be complicated, performative or disconnected from ordinary life. Her work is grounded in the idea that small moments of awareness — how we breathe before a difficult conversation, how we transition out of a demanding workday, how we notice tension or how we create space for reflection — can meaningfully change the way we experience our lives.

Her facilitation style is calm, generous and highly adaptable. She creates environments where participants are invited rather than pressured, allowing people with very different levels of experience to take part comfortably.

Philosophy

Wellbeing that belongs in real life

I believe wellbeing is most useful when it can leave the workshop room with you.

My work is less about temporarily escaping everyday life and more about helping people develop practical ways of meeting it differently.

That might mean learning how to recognise when your nervous system is becoming overloaded. It might mean discovering a breathing practice that helps you transition between meetings. It might mean creating a more intentional start to your morning, developing a healthier relationship with rest or simply becoming better at noticing what you need.

I don't believe there is one universal formula for wellbeing.

Different bodies, personalities, cultures, workplaces and stages of life require different approaches. My role as a facilitator is to create enough structure for people to feel supported while leaving enough space for genuine individual experience.

Signature Workshop

The Art of the Reset

A practical workshop for nervous-system regulation, presence and sustainable performance.

Modern life rarely gives us clear transitions.

We move from meeting to meeting, screen to screen and responsibility to responsibility, often carrying the physiological residue of one experience directly into the next.

The Art of the Reset is an experiential workshop exploring how short intentional pauses can influence attention, energy and the way we relate to pressure.

Participants learn how to notice early signals of activation and experiment with practical ways of creating a reset without needing to leave their workplace, dramatically change their schedule or adopt an elaborate wellness routine.

The experience includes

Arrival & Awareness
A guided check-in designed to help participants notice physical sensations, attention and current energy levels.

Understanding the Stress Response
An accessible introduction to how the nervous system responds to perceived demands.

Breath as a Tool
Participants explore several simple breathing patterns and experience how changes in breathing can alter their sense of pace and attention.

Movement & Release
Gentle, accessible movement designed for a broad range of bodies and abilities.

The Transition Practice
A practical two-to-five-minute sequence that participants can use between meetings, after commuting or before moving from work into home life.

Reflection
Participants identify their personal signs of overload and design a simple reset ritual they can continue using after the session.

Workshop details

Duration: 90 minutes
Extended version: 2.5 hours
Participants: 6–60
Delivery: In person or virtual
Suitable for: Corporate teams, leadership groups, retreats, conferences and private groups
Experience level: No previous meditation, breathwork or movement experience required

Participants are always encouraged to work within their own comfort level, and every practice can be adapted or skipped.

My Approach to Facilitation

Invitational

Participants are never forced to close their eyes, share personal information or participate in a practice that doesn't feel appropriate for them.

Accessible

Practices are explained clearly and without unnecessary jargon.

Experiential

People learn through doing rather than listening to long presentations.

Adaptive

Sessions can be adjusted to suit different physical abilities, experience levels, environments and organisational cultures.

Human

There is room for seriousness, curiosity, humour and imperfection.

A Typical Corporate Engagement

A typical engagement begins with a short briefing conversation to understand the organisation, audience, timing and desired outcome.

From there, a session is adapted to the group.

For example, a leadership offsite might include:

8:30 AM — Arrival Practice
15 minutes of grounding and intentional transition.

11:30 AM — Reset Session
A short breathing and mobility practice between strategy discussions.

4:00 PM — Integration Workshop
A facilitated reflective session helping the group transition from discussion into action.

For longer retreats, wellbeing practices can be woven throughout the program rather than delivered as a single standalone activity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do participants need experience with breathwork or meditation?

No. Most sessions are designed for mixed-experience groups, including complete beginners.

Do people need to change clothes?

Usually not. Most workplace sessions are intentionally designed so participants can take part in normal professional clothing.

Can sessions be delivered virtually?

Yes. Many of the workshops and shorter reset sessions can be adapted for online delivery.

Do you work with large organisations?

Yes. Programs can be adapted for intimate leadership groups as well as larger company-wide experiences.

Can a workshop be customised?

Yes. Content, language, timing and exercises can be adapted around the organisation and audience.

Do you work with individuals as well?

Yes. Alongside organisational work, Eliana offers private sessions, small-group experiences and retreats.

Is your work therapy?

No. These experiences are educational and wellbeing-focused and are not a replacement for medical, psychological or other clinical care.

Personal Note

I came to this work because I became fascinated by the difference between having a full life and actually being present for it.

Our culture gives us countless ways to optimise our schedules, increase our output and fill our attention.

Far fewer things teach us how to notice when enough is enough.

The practices I share are often incredibly simple. A breath. A pause. A walk without a phone. A moment of noticing where tension is sitting in the body.

None of these things removes the complexity of life.

But they can change the way we meet it.

That's what I hope people experience through my work: not an escape from their lives, but a deeper capacity to inhabit them.

— Eliana
